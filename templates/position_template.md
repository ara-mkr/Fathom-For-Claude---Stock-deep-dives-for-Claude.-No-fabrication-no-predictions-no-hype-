## Position management framing

Three frameworks. Not telling you which to apply.

**1. "Would you buy fresh today?"**
Forget your cost basis. If you had $X cash today and were starting from zero, would you buy [TICKER] at current price? Yes → consider adding or holding. No → consider trimming or selling.

The market doesn't know what you paid. Your cost basis is not a feature of the stock.

**2. "Has the thesis changed?"**
If the reason you bought is still true, hold. If it's no longer true, the loss is information — not a verdict on you. The mistake was the original purchase decision in light of what you knew then; the position now is a separate decision.

**3. "What's the opportunity cost?"**
Money tied up in a losing position can't be deployed elsewhere. Sometimes the better question is "what could this capital do somewhere else?"

## Cognitive errors to watch (offered, not asserted)

- **Anchoring** — refusing to sell below your entry price even though the entry is irrelevant to future performance
- **Sunk cost fallacy** — adding to a losing position only because you already own it
- **Disposition effect** — selling winners too early and holding losers too long
- **Confirmation bias** — only reading bullish analysis once you're long

If none of these apply to your situation, ignore them. They're patterns to check against, not accusations.
