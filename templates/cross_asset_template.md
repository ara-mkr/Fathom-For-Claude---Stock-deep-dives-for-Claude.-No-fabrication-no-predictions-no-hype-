## For proxy stocks (where the stock IS a vehicle for the asset)

If the stock primarily exists to give exposure to the asset (Bitcoin treasury companies → BTC, oil-services companies → crude, gold miners → gold):

**The actual question is: is the proxy a cleaner vehicle than the direct asset?**

| Factor | Direct asset | Proxy stock |
|---|---|---|
| Beta to underlying | 1.0× | Often 1.5-3.0× (operational + capital structure amplification) |
| Carrying cost | Storage / ETF expense ratio | Corporate overhead, share dilution |
| Daily decay (futures-based only) | Possible — contango | None — equity has no daily reset |
| Counterparty | Custodian / network | Operating company, creditors, sponsor |
| Tax | Asset-specific | Standard equity LTCG |

(Important nuance: a proxy stock with convertible debt against an asset balance is **structurally levered in capital-structure terms** — but does NOT have the daily-reset volatility decay of a leveraged futures ETF. Beta is amplified; it's not "leveraged" in the futures sense.)

## Cost and friction comparison

### [STOCK]
- Brokerage commissions (usually $0)
- Bid-ask spread (negligible for liquid names)
- Taxes on dividends and capital gains
- No carrying cost beyond opportunity cost

### [ASSET]
- For gold ETFs: expense ratio ~0.15-0.40% annually
- For physical gold: storage, insurance, dealer spreads
- For BTC ETFs: expense ratio ~0.20-1.50% annually
- For direct crypto: exchange fees, network fees, custody/security overhead
- For futures-based commodity ETFs: contango drag
